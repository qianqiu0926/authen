{"text":"Acne\nAcne is a common skin condition causing spots that can range from mild to severe. There are many treatment options and ways to manage acne.\nSymptoms of acne\nAcne can range from mild, with a few spots, to severe, with deep, painful lumps under your skin. It usually affects your face, chest and back.\nSymptoms of acne can include:\noily or greasy skin\nspots on your skin\nlarge, sometimes painful lumps under your skin\nchanges to your skin colour and texture, or scars where you've had spots\nskin that may feel hot or painful to touch\nHaving acne can also affect your mental health, causing anxiety, depression and low self-esteem.\nTypes of acne spots\nThere are different types of spots, and you may have more than 1 type.\nWhiteheads are small bumps that are the same colour as your skin.\nCredit: Richard Usatine MD / Science Photo Library: https://www.sciencephoto.com/media/1399720/view\nBlackheads are black and brown spots that are usually flat but may be raised.\nCredit: Richard Usatine MD / Science Photo Library: https://www.sciencephoto.com/media/1401494/view\nPapules are raised bumps. On white skin they look pink or red, on brown and black skin they may look brown or purple.\nCredit: CID / Science Photo Library: https://www.sciencephoto.com/media/1320347/view\nPustules are raised bumps filled with pus.\nCredit: Andreea Constantinescu: https://www.alamy.com/caucasian-female-with-cystic-acne-from-clogged-pores-and-oily-skin-image607160017.html?imageid=817DD6E0-C7F1-4903-995E-F0914399DFB8&pn=1&searchId=f3dd700af58f2f8d47b18d4f40254606&searchtype=0\nNodules and cysts are larger lumps under your skin. They may join together and can be painful.\nCredit: Richard Usatine MD / Science Photo Library: https://www.sciencephoto.com/media/1286230/view\nAcne can leave scars, such as shallow holes in your skin (ice-pick or boxcar scars).\nCredit: Mediscan: https://www.alamy.com/stock-photo-acne-scarring-52250835.html?imageid=61ACB5D3-34F2-4D93-AFE2-98B5634DF27F&pn=1&searchId=d3cc41840352e44af570e6a45a1dbb10&searchtype=9\nInformation:\nFind out moreAcne types (British Association of Dermatologists Acne Support)\nThings you can do to help with acne\nThere are some things you can do that can help with acne.\nDo\nwash your skin twice a day with a gentle cleanser\nremove make-up at the end of the day\nuse skincare and make-up products that are non-comedogenic (this means they will not clog pores)\ntry acne treatments you put on your skin such as salicylic acid or benzoyl peroxide – you can speak to a pharmacist for advice\nwear loose, breathable clothing (especially when exercising or sweating) if you have acne on your body\nDon’t\ndo not pick or squeeze spots because this can cause scars\ndo not scrub your skin as this can irritate it and make acne worse\nA pharmacist can help with acne\nA pharmacist can recommend treatments for acne, including:\ntreatments you put on your skin\nsupplements that come as tablets, such as zinc, niacinamide and vitamin A\nFind a pharmacy\nInformation:\nFind out more\nTreatment (British Association of Dermatologists Acne Support)\nPrevention (British Association of Dermatologists Acne Support)\nWhen and where to get medical help\nYou may need to get help from a GP for acne. They can offer treatment and advice to help manage it.\nNon-urgent advice: See a GP if:\nYou have acne and:\nyou've tried treating it yourself but it's not getting better\nyou have lots of spots, or the spots are deep, painful or causing scars\nyou have signs of a skin infection, such as hot, swollen and red skin (redness can be harder to see on brown and black skin)\nit's affecting your mental health\nA GP may offer treatments you can only get with a prescription or refer you to a skin specialist (dermatologist).\nTreatments from a GP or specialist\nThere are many acne treatments available from a GP or skin specialist (dermatologist) with a prescription. They are stronger than products you can buy from pharmacies and shops and can be very effective for more severe acne.\nMedicine you put on your skin\nAcne medicines you put on your skin (topical) usually come as a cream, gel or lotion. They're used to:\nreduce inflammation and oiliness, and prevent spots, such as the retinoid medicines tretinoin and adapalene\nkill the bacteria that cause inflammation and pus in acne, such as the antibiotics clindamycin and erythromycin\nMedicine you take\nAcne medicines you take usually come as a tablet that you swallow. They're used to:\ncontrol the type of bacteria that cause inflammation and pus in acne, such as the antibiotics doxycycline and lymecycline\nmanage hormone levels that contribute to acne, such as the combined pill\nreduce inflammation and oiliness, and prevent spots, such as the retinoid isotretinoin\nInformation:\nFind out more\nTreatment (British Association of Dermatologists Acne Support)\nCauses of acne\nAcne can happen when hair follicles (where hair grows on the skin) become blocked with excess oil and dead skin cells. Bacteria can grow in blocked hair follicles and cause inflammation and pus.\nAcne can be caused by:\nchanges in hormone levels, such as during puberty, periods, menopause and pregnancy\nconditions such as polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS) and rosacea\ntaking certain medicines\nproducts you use on your skin or hair that can make your skin greasy\nInformation:\nFind out more\nCauses (British Association of Dermatologists Acne Support)\nPage last reviewed: 22 September 2026\nNext review due: 22 September 2029","title":"Acne - NHS","url":"https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/acne/","author":"https://www.facebook.com/nhswebsite/"}
